Run under the VM rules:

## Why this probe exists

`get_contact_charge(top)` fell by a factor of 9577 across a 16× z
refinement while the field was converged to four digits. The plausible
explanation — that the defect is in DEVSIM's contact assembly — needs an
independent electrode charge to compare against. This probe computes three
of them and reports which converge.

## What converges

**The half-space mobile charge.** `q_half_top` and `q_half_bot` track each
other to 4–5 digits at every n_z (they must: the mobile charge is neutral
on a whole device). From n_z=84 to 5376 they move by 3.4 % / 3.3 % and the
increments shrink ~4× per factor-4 refinement, i.e. first order, tending to
about +2.303e-17 C. This is a VOLUME integral: it does not touch the
contact assembly at all. It is the independent measurement the pass was
for, and it is well behaved.

**Nothing else.**

- `q_contact` is still falling at n_z=5376 (7.134e-21 against 8.674e-21 at
  1344 — a 1.2 % step at 4× refinement, so it is approaching something, but
  not 2.3e-17).
- `q_full` (mobile charge over the whole domain) falls 21× then keeps
  falling; it is the difference of two 2.3e-17 terms, so its own convergence
  is limited by cancellation, not by physics. It is not an electrode charge
  either.
- `eps·E·A_disk` is 8 orders of magnitude below everything else and falls
  with n_z. This is **not** the electrode charge and must not be quoted as
  one (see the mechanism note below).

## Mechanism: what the contact flux is actually doing

Two facts from the probe output pin this down.

1. **The contact region field is ~5 orders below the junction field.**
   `E_boundary` is 3.4e-3 V/cm against 1.29e5 V/cm at the junction. The
   transport axis is DEVSIM's `y` (the mesh maps `z_lines` to `dir="y"`;
   `z` is constant at 0.0 and reading it — my first attempt — gives a
   one-plane mesh). One-sided `(V_contact − V_first_node)/(h/2)` and
   two-sided `(V_last − V_second_last)/h` differ by exactly 2 (7.404e-3
   vs 3.702e-3; 3.536e-3 vs 1.768e-3; 3.443e-3 vs 1.7216e-3), so the
   profile is locally linear at the contact and the boundary average is the
   right one to use.

2. **`contactcharge_edge` is a contact-node model that cancels the external
   bias.** In the contact equation
   `get_contact_equation_command` reports `node_charge_model=topnodemodel`,
   which charges the contact nodes for the applied bias. Its sum over the
   ALL silicon edges (`flux_edges`) is −3.681227e-12 C, identical to twelve
   digits at n_z = 84, 336, 1344 and 5376 — the signature of a model whose
   value does not depend on the mesh at all. Summed over the contact's own
   edge list via `get_contact_charge` it becomes mesh-dependent, because the
   contact edge list is what changes with n_z.

   Consequence: `get_contact_charge(top)` is the residual contact-node
   charge after the external bias is cancelled against the silicon at the
   junction. It is not "the charge on the electrode", and on this device it
   is not the quantity that changes when the device charges either. The
   electrode charge is `q_half_top`; the electrode's response is
   `q_half_top(V) − q_half_top(0)`.

### The arithmetic, shown

A capacitance needs the SAME mesh at two biases:
`dQ/dV = (q(V=0.35) − q(V=0.15)) / (0.35 − 0.15)`.

| n_z  | q_half_top(0.35) | q_half_top(0.15) | dQ/dV (F)   | q_contact(0.35) | q_contact(0.15) | dQ/dV (F)   |
|------|------------------|------------------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|
| 84   | +2.224817e-17    | +2.309865e-17    | −4.2524e-18 | +1.534052e-20   | +8.371079e-21   | +3.4847e-20 |
| 1344 | +2.297009e-17    | +2.382134e-17    | −4.2563e-18 | +7.327143e-21   | +3.972148e-21   | +1.6775e-20 |
| 5376 | +2.300605e-17    | +2.385730e-17    | −4.2563e-18 | +7.133901e-21   | +3.867303e-21   | +1.6333e-20 |

**Sign convention.** The probe computes `q_half_top = −q·Σ(p−n)·V` over
`z > 0`, which is the p side. Forward bias raises `p` there, so `Σ(p−n)`
grows, so `−q·Σ(p−n)` FALLS. The derivative is therefore negative by
construction. **The capacitance is the MAGNITUDE, 4.256e-18 F.** The sign is
a property of my probe's summation order, not of the device; it is stated
here so nobody re-derives "negative capacitance" from this table a third
time.

### What the two quantities actually are

- **`q_half_top` (fixed-mesh dQ/dV = −4.256e-18 F, magnitude 4.26e-18 F)**
  is converged: 84 → 1344 → 5376 changes the magnitude by 0.09 % then 0.00 %.
  It is a well-behaved volume integral that never touches the contact
  assembly.
- **`q_contact` (+1.68e-20 F at 1344, +1.63e-20 F at 5376)** is POSITIVE and
  converged to 2.6 % between those two meshes. It is **not** sign-violating.
  It is a POOR capacitance quantity — about 61× below `q_half_top` — and it
  tracks the mesh. The earlier "negative dQ/dV / a capacitance cannot be
  negative" reading was wrong: that number came from subtracting two
  different meshes at a fixed bias, which is not a capacitance.

### The reference was the wrong yardstick (see next section)

`C_pp = 8.678486e-19 F` is arithmetically CORRECT for this device
(`eps_s·π·r²/L`, `L = 6.0e-5 cm` is the true box length) but physically the
wrong reference: it is a **600 nm box** parallel plate, while the junction it
is being compared against has a depletion width of ~80–95 nm. Comparing the
two is why a "deficit" ever appeared. Against the depletion capacitance the
agreement is different in kind and is given below. **No "5×" and no "20–54×"
residual exists once the reference is right.**

## The correct reference: depletion capacitance `C_dep = eps_s·A/W(V)`

Inputs, all read from the code that builds this exact device
(`scripts/elec28_capacitance_fixture.py`):

| input        | value            | source                                   |
|--------------|------------------|------------------------------------------|
| `N_A`, `N_D` | 1.0e17 cm^-3     | `N_A = N_D = 1.0e17` (fixture line 51)   |
| `n_i`        | 1.0e10 cm^-3     | `N_I = 1.0e10` (fixture line 52)         |
| `eps_s`      | 11.7 (rel.)      | `EPS_S = 11.7` (fixture line 53)         |
| `A`          | π·(4e-6 cm)²     | `R_MAX_M = 4.0e-8` (fixture line 55)     |
| `T`          | 300 K            | `T=300.0` default, `_solve_at_bias` path |
| `V_bi`       | 0.833370 V       | computed, matches 2.12's recorded value  |

Derived: `W = sqrt(2·eps_s·(V_bi − V)/(q·N_A))`, `C_dep = eps_s·A/W`.

| V (V) | W (nm) | C_dep (F)   | q_half_top magnitude | ratio |
|-------|--------|-------------|----------------------|-------|
| 0.15  | 94.00  | 5.5392e-18  | 4.256e-18            | 0.77  |
| 0.35  | 79.06  | 6.5862e-18  | 4.256e-18            | 0.65  |

The measured capacitance is **0.65–0.77 of the depletion capacitance** and
is FLAT across bias where `C_dep` rises 19 %, so it does not reproduce the
depletion slope. Cause unknown; recorded, not explained. This is a far
better-posed statement than the old "5× low" and it rests on a converged
volume integral.

The superseded value `C_pp = 8.678486e-19 F` is retained only as a labelled
geometric constant of the mesh (`eps_s·π·r²/L` with the true box length); it
is NOT a capacitance of this junction and must not be used as the yardstick
again.

## What this changes in the record

- The 20–54× capacitance gap, the 2.13× bias slope and every absolute Q/C
  in the phase-2 blocks stay UNQUOTABLE: they measured `q_contact` against a
  box-length reference.
- **Task 2.8's charge balance cannot be closed on this device as built.**
  The transient path records `q = _contact_charge_coulombs`, the mobile
  charge over the WHOLE domain — which the probe shows is a difference of
  two ~2.3e-17 terms and therefore a cancellation, not a measurement. The
  balance needs `q_half_top` (one side), computed from node volumes that
  this probe shows converge. That is a code change to `transient_pulse`,
  NOT made here: it changes what the function reports and the lead's order
  says do not touch the balance until the probe is settled.
- `junction_capacitance` / the fixture should compute C from
  `q_half_top`, not `get_contact_charge`. Also not changed here.
- **OPEN OBSERVATION, not a cause:** `q_dop_net` =
  `q·Σ(p−n−NetDoping)·V` is **+4.261067e-20 C bit-identically at BOTH
  0.15 V and 0.35 V** (the reviewer confirmed this on an independent run).
  0.2 V of forward bias moves no net charge anywhere in the device, to all
  seven printed digits. For a neutral diode this quantity should be near
  zero — and it is — but a value that does not move under bias is a solve or

  earlier draft read the approach to zero as a z = 0 discretisation defect,
